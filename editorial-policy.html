EDITORIAL STANDARDS

Editorial policy

How we approach technical explanations, source verification, corrections, and links to further learning.

Accuracy and context

Enterprise software capabilities can depend on product release, license, security permissions, integrations, and tenant configuration. Our articles aim to explain broad concepts and troubleshooting logic. They should not be treated as universal step-by-step instructions for every environment.

Source verification

When an article discusses product-specific behavior or a procedure that may change, readers should confirm it against current official vendor documentation and their organization's change-control practices. We avoid inventing release-specific details where they have not been verified.

Originality and usefulness

Articles should answer a defined learning question, provide meaningful explanation or a usable checklist, and link to related pages where that helps the reader. Repetitive pages created only to target similar keywords are not the intended standard.

External links

External links are included when they provide relevant documentation or further-learning context. A link does not constitute a guarantee of a provider's services, outcomes, or claims. Commercial or training pages should be reviewed directly by readers for current details.

Corrections

Readers can report a suspected error using the GitHub issue tracker. Include the article URL, the statement in question, and a reliable source where possible. Do not post confidential information.

Limitations

Content is educational and may become outdated. Always test changes safely and consult qualified stakeholders before modifying production systems.
