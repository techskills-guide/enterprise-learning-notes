Workday Adaptive Planning: Models, Versions, and Forecast Cycles

A planning model is more than a collection of spreadsheets: it represents business assumptions, ownership, timing, and the relationships used to turn inputs into a forecast.

Conceptual learning guide · Validate capabilities against your licensed configuration

Define the planning question first

Before creating a model, identify the decisions it should support. Revenue planning, workforce expense, operating costs, and cash forecasts may require different drivers, dimensions, granularity, and owners. Clear business questions prevent unnecessary model complexity.

Models and dimensions

A model organizes the inputs, calculations, and outputs required for a planning process. Dimensions and organizational structures provide context for slicing data, such as department, region, product, or cost center where supported. Use only the level of detail needed for decisions and reporting.

Versions and scenarios

Planning teams often maintain distinct versions or scenarios for budgets, working forecasts, and alternative assumptions. Agree on naming, ownership, edit permissions, and the rules for copying or refreshing data between versions. A version should communicate its purpose and status clearly to users.

Drivers and assumptions

Driver-based planning links outcomes to business inputs such as headcount, unit volume, rates, or timing assumptions. Make the calculation logic understandable, identify who owns each assumption, and document how actual results feed the next forecast. Where possible, separate source facts from manually maintained assumptions.

Forecast-cycle controls

A reliable cycle defines deadlines, responsibilities, review stages, and validation checks. Teams should know when data refreshes, which version is authoritative, how late submissions are handled, and how changes are approved. Reconciliation to source systems and review of outliers help surface issues before publication.

Implementation checklist

  • Define the decisions and outputs the model must support.
  • Agree on dimensions, granularity, and source-system ownership.
  • Document key assumptions and calculation dependencies.
  • Set version naming, permissions, and review responsibilities.
  • Define data refresh timing, validation controls, and exception handling.
  • Test normal, boundary, and changed-assumption scenarios.
Configuration note: Product features and model design options can vary by licensed modules and implementation. Use current official Workday Adaptive Planning resources and your organization's design standards to confirm exact capabilities.
